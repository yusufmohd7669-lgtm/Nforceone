# Inner Pages Animation Spec — NForce One

**Global technical note (stated once, applies everywhere below):** every animation in this spec must be built from `transform` and `opacity` only (no layout-triggering properties), must respect `prefers-reduced-motion: reduce` by collapsing to instant or near-instant (≤100ms opacity-only) state changes with all signature moments disabled, and must never gate interactivity — an element is clickable/focusable/typeable the instant it exists in the DOM, regardless of what its entrance animation is still doing visually.

**Shared scan-sweep budget note:** the scan-sweep line is a sitewide asset capped at 3–4 uses total, homepage included. Treat inner pages as sharing a budget of roughly 1–2 remaining uses, not 1–2 *per page*. The strongest candidate is **/pega** (flagship hub, functions as a second hero). If a second inner-page sweep is available after accounting for the homepage's usage, the next-best candidate is **/about**'s "LEADERSHIP & ARCHITECTS" transition — but if in doubt, skip it and hold the line at one. Every other page below is written assuming it does **not** get a sweep.

---

## /pega

**(a) Signature moments that fit.** This is the flagship practice page and the highest-production-value inner page, so it earns the largest share of the signature palette. The hero headline "Enterprise Pega Implementations Built by Certified Lead Architects." should get the full masked-line reveal with expo easing — this is the one place on the page that deserves that weight. The six numbered capability cards (01–06) and the single featured case study card are clickable, so they inherit the sitewide HUD-bracket hover automatically — no extra decision needed there, just confirm it's wired the same as every other card site-wide. The case study card's stats (70% touchless claims, 14 cycle-time reduction) are a clean fit for digit-roll counters, each digit in its own rolling column, firing once when the card crosses the viewport threshold. If the shared scan-sweep budget allows one inner-page use, spend it here: trigger the sweep across the "PEGA PRACTICE CAPABILITIES" section header the moment it enters view, functioning as a visual "loading the practice spec" beat before the 01–06 grid populates.

**(b) New moment worth inventing.** Invent a "verification stamp" for the "Certified Delivery Team" badge panel: a small check/seal icon draws itself on via `stroke-dasharray` line-draw (roughly 0.4s, single pass, no loop) the first time the panel scrolls into view, as though a credential is being certified in real time. This is restrained, mechanical, and thematically load-bearing (it reinforces "certified") rather than decorative. Pair it with a lighter version of the same line-draw treatment on the underline of each "Explore Capability" link on hover — the underline traces in from left to right in ~150ms instead of simply fading, giving links a "targeting trace" feel consistent with the HUD language without inventing a whole new interaction class.

**(c) Baseline treatment for everything else.** The three nav chips (Pega Development/Testing/Implementation) should fade in with a very slight horizontal slide (4–6px) over 0.5s, staggered by ~60ms each — quick enough to read as a single beat, not a sequence. The three "OUR ARCHITECTURAL DISCIPLINE" principle blocks (Situational Layer Cake, ≥90 Guardrail Compliance, Automated Deployment Manager) should reveal their headline first and let the small tag label underneath land a beat later (~80ms delay), like a terminal printing a primary line followed by a sub-line — this small offset is enough to feel intentional without slowing the section down.

**(d) Don't do this.** Don't apply the masked-line reveal to all six capability card headlines — at 01–06 that's six expo-eased reveals stacking as someone scrolls, which reads as slow and repetitive on the flagship page that most needs to feel fast and confident; reserve masked-line for the hero only and let capability cards use the fast 0.5–0.6s baseline. Don't fire the scan-sweep more than once on this page, and don't let it re-trigger if the user scrolls the section out of view and back in — gate it with a "played once" flag. Don't let the digit-roll counters on the case study card re-roll every time the card re-enters the viewport on scroll-up/scroll-down; roll once, then hold the resolved value permanently.

## /industries

**(a) Signature moments that fit.** This page is essentially a stat showcase — four verticals × three stat callouts each is twelve numbers — so digit-roll counters are the obvious anchor moment here, more so than on any other inner page. Masked-line reveal belongs on the hero headline ("Domain-Specific Solutions for Regulated & High-Volume Sectors.") and, in a slightly lighter form, on each of the four panel headlines (Insurance, Banking & Financial Services, Healthcare & Life Sciences, Retail & eCommerce) since those function as sub-heroes for their section. The "Explore [Industry] Architecture" links should carry the sitewide magnetic-button behavior.

**(b) New moment worth inventing.** Give each panel's "VERTICAL SOLUTION" eyebrow tag a brief scramble-decode effect as the panel enters view: for roughly 250–300ms the tag cycles through randomized monospace characters before resolving into the real label, like a targeting system acquiring lock before it reads out the sector name. This is distinct from anything on the homepage, ties naturally into "regulated/high-volume sectors" as a targeting metaphor, and — critically — should be scoped to the eyebrow tag *only*, once per panel. Do not extend it to the stat labels or headline; one scramble-decode per panel entrance is the ceiling.

**(c) Baseline treatment for everything else.** Each panel's description paragraph does a fast, plain opacity fade (0.5s) directly under its headline. The three stat callouts within a panel should feel like a single row landing together — the digit-roll handles the number itself, while the stat's text label crossfades in alongside it rather than reveal-and-then-label sequencing, which would feel bureaucratic across twelve total stats.

**(d) Don't do this.** Don't run the scramble-decode on every stat label in addition to the eyebrow tag — four panels × three stats × a decode effect is a lot of flicker on a page a buyer is trying to quickly compare numbers on. Don't let each of the four panels run a heavy staggered multi-element entrance (headline, then tag, then description, then three stats, each offset) — a fast scroller will hit panel two before panel one finishes, and the compounding delays read as sluggish; keep total panel-entrance choreography under ~0.6s even though the digit-roll itself can run slightly longer for the number resolve. Don't spend a scan-sweep here if it's already used on /pega — this page's job is scannable stat density, not a moment of spectacle.

## /case-studies

**(a) Signature moments that fit.** The four case study cards are clickable, so HUD-bracket hover applies to them by the sitewide rule. Each card's two big stat numbers are a strong digit-roll fit. The hero headline gets the masked-line reveal, and that should be the *only* place on this page that gets it — everything below the filter needs to be fast and repeatable, not ceremonial.

**(b) New moment worth inventing — this is the important one for this page.** The "FILTER BY INDUSTRY" pill control needs its own precise selector mechanic: a thin red bar or bracket that snaps (not eases-and-settles, snaps — 150–200ms, mechanical) to sit under or around the active pill when clicked, like a targeting reticle locking onto a new selection rather than drifting to it. For the grid itself, build a dedicated "quick-swap" pattern that is explicitly *not* the scroll-reveal system: cards that stop matching the active filter exit via a fast opacity+slight-scale-down crossfade (~150–200ms), and cards that newly match enter via the mirror of that same crossfade — no restagger, no re-run of the masked-line or digit-roll systems, no grid-wide re-sequencing. This quick-swap pattern should be treated as its own component, separate from the page's scroll-triggered reveal logic, precisely because it needs to survive being clicked four times in a row without feeling like it's replaying a intro each time.

**(c) Baseline treatment for everything else.** Each card's industry tag and category tag do a fast 0.3s fade with no transform. "READ FULL SPEC & RESULTS" gets the sitewide magnetic-button pull on hover, nothing more.

**(d) Don't do this.** Don't replay the digit-roll counters every time a card reappears after a filter change — a stat should roll into place once, the first time that card is ever shown to this visitor, and hold its resolved value in state from then on; re-rolling "70%" every time someone clicks back to "All" will feel like a slot machine on a page meant to convey rigor. Don't animate the grid with per-card staggered entrance delays on filter clicks — that's the single biggest failure mode for this page, since a buyer comparing sectors will click through all four tabs in quick succession, and a slow staggered reveal on every click reads as the site struggling to keep up, not as polish. Don't extend masked-line/expo-eased text reveals to the card headlines inside the filtered grid — those headlines need to be part of the fast quick-swap crossfade, not a separate slower system layered on top.

## /about

**(a) Signature moments that fit.** Masked-line reveal belongs on the hero headline ("Revolutionizing Businesses Through Transformative Technology Solutions.") and on the two section headers, "OUR OPERATING PRINCIPLES" and "LEADERSHIP & ARCHITECTS" — these read as genuine section titles, not card copy, so the full-weight treatment is earned. This page is light on hard numbers, so digit-roll's core use case (stat counters) is largely absent here; instead, repurpose the digit-roll *mechanic* at small scale (see below) rather than forcing in a stat that doesn't exist. Whether HUD-bracket applies to the four principle cards or six team member cards depends entirely on whether they're actual links/click-throughs — see the warning below, because this is the page most likely to misapply that hover state.

**(b) New moment worth inventing.** Give each team member's certifications/credentials list a "personnel file" reveal: when a team card scrolls into view, the credential tags print in sequentially, one at a time, ~40–60ms apart, monospace, like a manifest or clearance dossier being printed line by line rather than all fading in together. This gives the bios a distinct identity from every other card list on the site and fits the HUD/systems aesthetic (a "credentials clearance" read) without inventing something ornamental. Separately, for the four numbered principle cards, let the number itself (01–04) digit-roll from 00 up to its real value on scroll-in — this is a low-stakes, non-stat use of the same mechanic that keeps the site's visual language consistent even on a page without real statistics to count up.

**(c) Baseline treatment for everything else.** Bio paragraphs do a plain 0.5s opacity fade. Name and title use a lighter clip-mask reveal (not the full masked-line expo treatment — reserve that for section headers only). The final CTA's two buttons (Book a Consultation / View Career Openings) get the standard sitewide magnetic-button behavior, nothing page-specific.

**(d) Don't do this.** Don't apply the HUD-bracket corner-reticle hover to the six team member cards or four principle cards unless they are genuinely clickable (e.g., link out to a full bio or a principle detail) — that hover state is a strong "this is interactive" signal sitewide, and putting it on an inert bio card will read to a careful enterprise buyer as a broken or dead link, which is a worse outcome than under-animating. Don't stagger-reveal all six team cards one-by-one with individual delays — six sequential 0.5s+ reveals compound into well over two seconds of settling time; reveal them by row (two rows of three, one stagger step per row) or cap total stagger under ~1.2s. Don't let the personnel-file credential-print effect re-trigger on every hover — it should fire once on scroll-into-view only, with hover reserved for a simple, static highlight state.

## /insights

**(a) Signature moments that fit.** This is the lightest page on the site content-wise, so it should also be the most restrained in signature-moment usage — resist the urge to make it "interesting" with more motion than the content warrants. Masked-line reveal on the hero headline only. The three article cards are clickable, so HUD-bracket hover applies to them per the sitewide rule. "READ ARTICLE" gets the standard magnetic-button treatment.

**(b) New moment worth inventing.** Since this page has no real stats to anchor a digit-roll counter to, repurpose the mechanic in miniature for texture: the read-time indicator (e.g., "6 MIN READ") can tick up digit-by-digit very quickly (under 0.3s total) when a card enters view, like a countdown/readout initializing — small, fast, and functional-feeling rather than decorative. Separately, let each card's hashtag-style topic tags populate as a fast sequential stagger (~50ms per tag), like metadata fields filling in on a spec sheet — this reinforces the site's bracketed-tag identity without adding a new visual language.

**(c) Baseline treatment for everything else.** Category tag, headline, description, and topic tags each use a fast 0.5–0.6s reveal, varied slightly per element type — e.g., the category tag can slide in 4px from the left while the description is a pure opacity fade with no transform — so the card doesn't feel like one uniform block animating in unison.

**(d) Don't do this.** Don't run the full masked-line treatment on all three article headlines — that's landing-page-grade weight on what is functionally a content index; reserve it for the hero only. Don't spend any of the scan-sweep budget here — there's no natural threshold moment on this page worth it, and every sweep used elsewhere makes the ones that remain feel less special. Don't let the hashtag-tag stagger delay the "READ ARTICLE" link's clickability even by a frame — the tag animation is cosmetic and must never be perceived as blocking interaction.

## /careers

**(a) Signature moments that fit.** Masked-line reveal on the hero headline only. The three "CURRENT OPENINGS" job cards are the clickable, decision-relevant unit on this page, so they get the sitewide HUD-bracket hover; the four "ENGINEERING CULTURE" perk cards are informational, not click-throughs, so they should generally sit outside that treatment (see warning below unless product decides to make them link out to a culture page). Digit-roll's natural home here is small but real: the "(3)" in "View Open Positions (3)" can tick into place on load, and each job card's years-of-experience figure (e.g., "5+ Years") can use the same digit column mechanic for consistency.

**(b) New moment worth inventing.** Give the "Actively Hiring" status badge on each job card a small, continuously pulsing status dot — a slow, subtle opacity breathing cycle (roughly 2s ease in/out, looping) rather than a one-time reveal — functioning like a live system indicator ("this role is currently open") rather than decoration. This is one of the few places on the site where an ambient/looping animation is appropriate, precisely because it's communicating real, live status rather than just announcing arrival. Separately, treat the "key responsibilities" bullet list inside each job card as a spec sheet: each line is prefixed with a small bracket marker (e.g., `[>]`) that draws in and the line text follows, staggered fast (~40ms per line) — this reinforces the job card as a "role spec" rather than generic marketing copy.

**(c) Baseline treatment for everything else.** Department tag and employment-type tag fade in fast (0.3–0.4s) with no transform. Job title uses a lighter clip-reveal (not the full masked-line treatment — see warning). Description text is a plain opacity fade.

**(d) Don't do this.** Don't apply the full masked-line hero treatment to the three job titles — a candidate evaluating roles will scan this page multiple times over days, and a heavy expo-eased reveal on every visit for content they've already read becomes actively annoying; keep job cards on the fast baseline. Don't make the "Actively Hiring" pulse fast or high-contrast — it needs to be slow and quiet enough to sit in peripheral vision without competing with the candidate actually reading the responsibilities list. If a department or role-type filter is added to "CURRENT OPENINGS" apply the exact same quick-swap crossfade pattern specified for /case-studies rather than re-running card entrance animations on every click — job seekers filter repeatedly and will feel any lag immediately, and none of the per-card signature moments (bracket-draw responsibilities, digit-roll years) should re-trigger on a filter change, only on true first appearance.

## /contact

**(a) Signature moments that fit.** This should be the quietest page on the entire site — it exists to convert, and every animation choice here should be judged against "does this make submitting the form easier or does it just look nice," with a strong bias toward the former. The only signature moment that clearly belongs is the masked-line reveal on the hero headline. Do not add HUD-bracket to the contact info cards (phone, email, location, response guarantee) unless they are genuinely interactive (click-to-call, click-to-email) — if they're static informational cards, they should sit on the fast baseline like everything else on this page.

**(b) New moment worth inventing.** The "Service of Interest" dropdown (13 options) should expand with a fast, precise clip-path reveal — roughly 150ms, top-down, no bounce or overshoot, options appearing as a single resolved block rather than staggering item-by-item (staggering thirteen items would be slow and would read as the form control being sluggish). The Submit button should carry a "transmitting" state: on submit, the label swaps to something like "TRANSMITTING…" with a minimal opacity/transform-only progress indicator (three cycling dots, or a thin line sweeping the button's width) — this is thematically related to the scan-sweep's visual language but is a functional loading state on a button, not a decorative section-entrance, so it should not be counted against the sitewide sweep budget. On success, swap directly to a confirmation state with a fast, clear opacity change — no confetti, no bounce, no delay for suspense. The "Confidentiality Assurance" NDA note block can get a single small seal-icon line-draw (echoing the certified-badge treatment suggested for /pega) once, on scroll into view, to visually reinforce that this is a formal, trustworthy assurance rather than boilerplate legal text.

**(c) Baseline treatment for everything else.** The contact info cards (phone, email, Dallas-Fort Worth location, response guarantee) do a fast fade-slide (0.5s, ~4px), staggered lightly (~60ms apart) as a group. The form container itself may fade in once on page load, but this must not delay any individual field's interactivity — the fields are focusable and typeable from the first frame regardless of what the container's own fade is doing.

**(d) Don't do this.** Don't apply any entrance animation, opacity ramp, or transform-based delay to the form inputs themselves that could make them not-yet-focusable or not-yet-typeable on load — this should be explicitly tested by tabbing into the first field and typing immediately after page load, before any animation has visually settled. Don't stagger the 13-option dropdown list item-by-item — it must resolve as one fast block. Don't add decorative validation feedback (shaking an invalid field, a bouncy error toast, a color pulse) — inline validation errors should appear and disappear via a fast, flat opacity change (~150ms) with no motion, consistent with the site's "precise engineering firm" tone; a shake or bounce on a form error is exactly the kind of gimmick this brand identity is built to avoid.
